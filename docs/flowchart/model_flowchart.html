One coupling interval, repeated for the whole run

Gas chemistry and aerosol take turns (operator splitting). In each interval Δtc (5 min in Levels 1–2, 15 min in Level 3, 1 min with nucleation on) the chemistry runs first, its products go to the aerosol model, and the vapors the particles took up or released go back to the gas. PPM marks where particles move between size bins with the piecewise-parabolic method.

Scenario

initial gases: limonene, O₃, NO, NO₂, SO₂, CO, CH₄ …
seed aerosol: number, size, sulfate / organic (goes straight to step 3)
T, RH (constant for the run); site and date (Level 3)
γ(N₂O₅); c* table (Li et al. 2016 for species not in it); number of size bins (40–320)

What the particles carry

  • sulfate: the seed plus condensed H₂SO₄
  • POA: the seed's non-volatile organic; absorbs SOA
  • SOA: 15 volatility classes; a per-species tracker shows which products they hold
  • nitrate: only from N₂O₅ uptake (non-volatile)
  • water: in equilibrium with RH
Not in the model: ammonium nitrate and NH₃, HNO₃ partitioning, sea salt, dust; dilution, deposition and wall loss.

The three levels

  • Level 1, Volatility: no chemistry (steps 1, 2 and 4 are skipped). A surrogate organic with the c* you choose is produced straight into one class; H₂SO₄ at a production rate you choose.
  • Level 2, Limonene SOA: full chemistry, constant light, 6 h.
  • Level 3, Day and night: TUV-x light over 24 h, N₂O₅ uptake (γ) makes nitrate at night, NO₂ reset to 1 ppb every interval Δtc (15 min; 1 min with nucleation on).

Clocks and grids

  • coupling interval Δtc: 300 s (Levels 1–2), 900 s (Level 3)
  • aerosol micro-step Δtm: 60 s; vbs-jax sub-steps inside it
  • with nucleation on: Δtm = 5 s, and the requested interval Δtreq is cut into m = ⌈Δtreq / 60 s⌉ equal parts, Δtc = Δtreq / m (60 s for 300, 600 or 900 s); output times unchanged
  • size bins: 40 (default), 80, 160 or 320, always 1.7 nm to 17.5 µm at sulfate density (plots: 1.8 nm to 19 µm)
  • output: every 10 min (Levels 1–2), 30 min (Level 3)
  • all in float64 JAX; one compile per grid and level

Inside one aerosol micro-step (60 s; 5 s with nucleation on)

Step 3 on page 1, in the order the model runs it. Each step changes the size distribution (number and mass of each species in each size bin) before the next one starts.
1
Sources. The H₂SO₄ vapor C gets the micro-step's production at once, C ← C + P·Δtm (P the rate from the chemistry, Δtm the micro-step). Nitrate from N₂O₅ uptake is added to the particles, each size bin getting its share of the uptake (larger surface, larger share); the particles that grew are then moved to the bins that match their new mass PPM.
Then MNFIX MNFIX keeps each bin's mean mass inside the bin. Level 1: the surrogate vapor and H₂SO₄ come from your production rates.
2
Nucleation. New 1.7 nm particles from H₂SO₄ and water at the rate J = k(T)·Cn, n ≈ 4, k(T) larger in colder air (the binary neutral term of Dunne et al. 2016; NH₃, ions and organics set to zero); as in TOMAS each new particle is 10 % organic by mass. Off in the presets; it needs high H₂SO₄ and cold air.
Why 5 s: C jumps by P·Δtm at each micro-step's start and J grows as C⁴, so 60 s steps put the peak particle number up to 78 % too high; 5 s is within 4 % (40 bins). Sub-steps Δts keep J·Δts ≤ N/2 (N the total particle number); MNFIX afterwards MNFIX.
3
Coagulation. Particles collide and stick: number falls, mass stays. 40 bins: the two-moment scheme of Fortran TOMAS (Tzivion, Feingold and Levin 1987); 80–320 bins: the linear discrete method (Simmel et al. 2002).
Ten forward-Euler sub-steps x ← x + (Δtm/10)·dx/dt, x each bin's number and mass (6 s each; 0.5 s with nucleation on), with the collision kernel fixed over Δtm; MNFIX after each MNFIX.
4
H₂SO₄ condensation. The vapor condenses onto every bin at the kinetic rate (Fuchs–Sutugin correction in Dahneke's form, accommodation 1); it never evaporates. Particles grow across bin edges PPM.
Flux-form PPM in log-mass with a monotone limiter (Colella and Woodward 1984); MNFIX before and after MNFIX, then particle water is reset.
5
SOA partitioning (vbs-jax). For each volatility class and size bin: condensation where the vapor exceeds the bin's equilibrium vapor, evaporation where it is below. Equilibrium vapor = c* (corrected to the run temperature) × the class's share of the organic mass (Raoult, by mass) × the Kelvin term (σ = 0.025 N m⁻¹). Seed POA and SOA absorb the vapor; a particle with no organic yet still takes up vapor above its c* (absorbing floor, ASSUMPTIONS AC1). Then each bin's particles move by their own growth or shrinkage PPM.
vbs-jax adaptive sub-steps with a Raoult fixed-point loop; the PPM remap carries each bin's parabolic sub-bin profile and its composition to the bins that match the new masses; organic that would push the top bin off the grid stays in the gas; MNFIX keeps a tiny floor in empty bins MNFIX.
6
Water. Particle water in equilibrium with RH: ammonium-bisulfate water ratio for sulfate and, as a simplification, for organics and nitrate too (organics come out too hygroscopic; ASSUMPTIONS A9).

What PPM does here

A sectional model keeps particles in fixed size bins. When particles grow, they must be moved to bigger bins without smearing the distribution or losing mass. PPM fits a parabola to the number in each bin (limited, so it never goes negative or overshoots), shifts it by the growth, and deposits it onto the fixed bins. Number and the mass of every species are conserved to round-off.
  • Used for: H₂SO₄ condensation, SOA growth and evaporation, nitrate added by N₂O₅ uptake.
  • Before PPM (tag aaar-stable-2026-10-15): SOA and nitrate were moved by MNFIX, which made the size distribution jagged; with PPM the mean diameter changes by under 0.6 % from 40 to 160 bins.

MNFIX, still used

TOMAS's bookkeeping fix (Adams and Seinfeld 2002): if a bin's mean particle mass has left the bin (after steps 1–5), part of the bin moves to the neighbouring bin so number and mass stay consistent. With PPM it only does small corrections; the old SOA and nitrate path (moved by MNFIX) is kept for comparison.
